# Source modes

Use the source that carries the intended meaning. Do not force every task through YouTube or transcription.

## Raw thought or typed notes

1. Separate the central claim, supporting points, examples, and desired reaction.
2. Preserve useful specificity and the author's uncertainty; do not make a rough thought sound more certain than it is.
3. Ask about audience or goal only when they cannot be inferred and would change the post.
4. Offer a structure or first draft. Do not publish unless explicitly asked and authorized.

Useful request:

```text
Вот сырая мысль. Разверни её в подробный Telegram-пост, сохрани мой смысл,
используй мой voice-profile.md и не публикуй.
```

## Voice note, local audio, podcast, or meeting

Transcribe locally with `scripts/transcribe_audio.py`. Keep the raw transcript as working evidence, then distinguish:

- the speaker's actual claims;
- examples and side remarks;
- uncertain recognition, names, or specialist terms;
- material that should remain private;
- ideas suitable for a public post.

Clean spoken repetition without flattening the speaker's logic. Never present a reconstructed phrase as a direct quote without checking the audio.

## Article URL, document, or pasted text

Read the primary source, record its title, author, URL, and verification date, then create a transformative post rather than a near-copy.

- Preserve links for claims that can change.
- Distinguish the source author's conclusions from the user's commentary.
- Quote sparingly and accurately.
- If the page cannot be accessed, ask for pasted text or a file; do not invent its contents.

## Video

For YouTube, follow `youtube-workflow.md`: metadata and captions first, audio only if needed, full video only for visual evidence or frame selection. For a third-party video, link and credit the original and avoid publishing a full transcript.

## Several sources

Build one evidence map and mark agreement, conflict, and uncertainty. Prefer current primary sources for changing facts. If sources conflict on a material point, surface the conflict instead of silently choosing the most convenient version.

## Questions that are worth asking

Ask only when an answer changes the output materially:

- Who exactly should act or change their mind?
- Is the purpose education, discussion, lead generation, sales, or announcement?
- Must all detail be preserved, or may the source be condensed?
- Which first-person position belongs to the user rather than the source author?
- What link or call to action is required?
- Is this only a draft, a private test, or an authorized production send?

Formatting preferences, headline variants, and minor length choices can usually be handled with a reasonable default and revised after the first draft.
